About关于我

I am a senior at Wellesley College, studying Mathematics and Women's and Gender Studies, and I am writing a thesis about what happens to Chinese-language queer women's content after it is posted.

The short version: a lot of it does not stay up. If you spend any time in these corners of Douyin you already know this — an account you followed stops showing up, a video you saved is not there when you go back, someone mentions that a post "没了" and nobody is surprised. What almost nobody has is a record of it. The posts that disappear take the evidence that they existed with them, so the disappearance leaves no trace to count.

So I have been keeping that record. Since September I have followed a set of publicly posted videos and 图文 day by day, noting which ones are still reachable and which are not. That gives me numbers — how many, how fast, which kinds. What it cannot give me is any of the part that matters: whether the person knew, whether they were told anything, what they did next, and whether it changed what they post now. A dataset can see that something went. Only the person who made it can say what that was like.

That is what the interviews are for, and why this page exists.

I work on this with Prof. Elena Creef (Women's and Gender Studies) and Prof. Eni Mustafaraj (Computer Science). Before it I spent a year on T/P/H labelling culture on Douyin, hand-coding a dataset of 600+ videos. I am also a research assistant at Yale with Prof. Emma Zang, where I run semi-structured interviews with Chinese respondents about family and fertility, and at the MIT Sloan Superminds Lab with Prof. Thomas Malone.

所以我在做这份记录。从九月开始，我每天跟踪一批公开发布的视频 和图文，看哪些还能打开、哪些打不开了。这能给我一些数字：有多少、 多快、什么样的内容。但它给不了真正要紧的那部分——当事人知不 知道、有没有收到任何说明、当时做了什么、之后发东西的方式有没有 因此改变。数据只能看到「某样东西没了」，只有发它的人能说出 那是什么感觉。

访谈就是为了这一部分，这个页面也是。

The study关于这项研究

Content Moderation, Removal, and Self-Censorship Among Chinese-Language Lesbian Video Creators

Two halves. One follows a set of publicly posted videos and 图文 day by day and records which ones are still reachable — that is where the numbers come from. The other is interviews with the creators, about posting, about moderation they have run into, and about the times they decided to take something down themselves.

Interviews run 15–20 minutes, on Tencent Meeting, WeChat, Zoom, or by text, whichever the person prefers, with ¥50 afterwards as a thank-you for the time. No real names are asked for or recorded; write-ups use pseudonyms. Participation is voluntary and can stop at any point.

Principal investigator: Prof. Eni Mustafaraj, Department of Computer Science, Wellesley College. Reviewed and approved by , protocol . Questions about the study, or about your rights as a participant, can go to me, to the PI, or to the Brandeis HRPP at hrpp@brandeis.edu.

中文拉拉短视频创作者所经历的内容审核、下架与自我审查

研究分两半。一半是长期追踪一批公开发布的视频和图文，每天记录 哪些还能打开——数字来自这里。另一半是和创作者的访谈：发内容的 经历、遇到过的审核情况，以及自己决定删掉某些内容时是怎么想的。

访谈大约 15 到 20 分钟，可以用腾讯会议、微信、Zoom，或者直接 打字聊，结束后会有 50 元作为对时间的一点心意。不会问、也不会 记录真名，写出来时用化名。参与完全自愿，随时可以停。
